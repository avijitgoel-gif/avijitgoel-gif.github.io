## How much resolution you actually need

Documents for upload (PAN, Aadhaar, bank statements) need only 200 or 300 dpi. Photos reprinted at the same size need 300 to 600 dpi. The 2400 or 4800 dpi headline only helps when you enlarge small photos.

Higher resolution also costs time. Epson's V39 II brochure lists an A4 colour scan at 10 seconds at 300 dpi, 29 seconds at 600 dpi and 103 seconds at 1200 dpi ([Epson V39II brochure](https://download.epson.com.sg/brochure/Scanners/SCANNER_V39II_4pp_Bro0223.pdf)).

### Scan settings for the 2 MB myAadhaar limit

UIDAI's free online document update on the myAadhaar portal has been extended to 14 June 2027, and uploads must be JPEG, PNG or PDF files below 2 MB ([India Today, 22 May 2026](https://www.indiatoday.in/information/story/uidai-free-aadhaar-update-service-extended-step-by-step-process-2915457-2026-05-22)).

The maths explains why a default scan can fail. An A4 page at 300 dpi is about 2,480 x 3,508 pixels, roughly 8.7 million pixels. At 24-bit colour that is about 26 MB before compression. So:

1. Scan at 200 or 300 dpi, not the scanner's maximum.
2. Use greyscale for black and white documents.
3. Save as JPEG or as a compressed PDF, not TIFF or BMP.
4. Check the file size before you upload.

3. **Price vs MRP.** Compare with Canon India's MRP and Epson's ₹6,999 launch price, not the Amazon "M.R.P." line.
