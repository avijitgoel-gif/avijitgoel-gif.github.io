## Why the 12% and 28% keys are out of date

On 3 September 2025 the GST Council recommended a two slab structure, and the Finance Ministry notified the new goods rates in Notification No. 9/2025-Central Tax (Rate), dated 17 September 2025, effective from 22 September 2025. The PIB note on the reform puts it simply: GST moved to a two slab structure of 5% and 18%, "removing the earlier 12% and 28% rates", with a 40% rate for luxury and sin goods such as pan masala, aerated drinks and high end cars.

A few exceptions remain. Cigarettes, chewing tobacco and beedi stay on their old rates and compensation cess until a later notified date. Specified bricks got a separate 12% rate under Notification No. 14/2025. Gold and diamonds keep their special low rates. So 12% and 28% are not gone for every single item, but for a typical kirana, stationery, electronics or garment shop they are now rarely used.

Here is the problem in plain numbers. Casio's manual for the MJ-12GST and MJ-120GST lists the factory default presets as GST+0 = 0%, GST+1 = 5%, GST+2 = 12%, GST+3 = 18% and GST+4 = 28%. If a staff member presses GST+4 out of habit on a ₹10,000 TV that is now taxed at 18%, the calculator adds ₹2,800 instead of ₹1,800.

## How to reset the GST keys (Casio MJ-120GST and MJ-12GST)

Casio's manual gives this sequence. It takes under a minute:

1. Press **AC**.
2. Hold down the **SET/%** key until "SET" appears.
3. Press the GST key you want to change, for example **GST+2**.
4. Type the new rate, for example **40**, and press **SET/%**.
5. Repeat for any other key. To check a rate, press **AC** and then that GST key.

A practical layout for most shops in 2026 is GST+0 = 0%, GST+1 = 5%, GST+2 = 18%, GST+3 = 40% and GST+4 for whatever special rate you sell most (or leave it at 18% so nobody can press a wrong rate).

Two warnings from the manual. First, replacing the battery, or blocking sunlight while the battery is low, can change or erase your rate settings, so recheck the rates after a battery change. Second, end a GST bill with a GST key operation, not with the = key, or the grand total recall will not work as expected.

On the Casio MJ-120D, which has one tax rate, the steps are: press **AC**, hold **% (RATE SET)** until "TAX %" appears, type the rate, then press **%** again. Casio's manual notes that the Memory Clear button on the back also wipes the tax rate.

## A worked GST example

Say you sell a ₹1,000 item at 18% within your state.

- **Adding tax (GST+ mode):** type 1000 and press the 18% key. The calculator shows ₹1,180 and lets you view the tax of ₹180.
- **Splitting the tax:** for an intra state sale, the 18% is split into 9% CGST and 9% SGST, so ₹90 each. The calculator shows total GST only, so halve it yourself.
- **Removing tax (TAX- mode):** a customer pays ₹1,180 inclusive. Switch to TAX- mode, type 1180 and press the 18% key. You get the base price of ₹1,000 and tax of ₹180.

On the MJ-120GST and MJ-12GST, the GST GT key then gives grand totals of net value, gross value and GST across a bill that mixes, say, 5% and 18% items, which is the main reason to buy a five key model over a single rate one.
