## Do you even need a stabilizer for a modern TV?

Often not, and you should know that before you buy.

India's declared household supply is 230 V at 50 Hz. Under Rule 54 of the Indian Electricity Rules, 1956, the supplier should keep low-voltage supply within 6 percent of the declared voltage, which works out to roughly 216 V to 244 V. In practice many areas see wider swings, especially on long rural feeders and during summer peak load.

Modern LED, QLED and OLED TVs use a switch mode power supply (SMPS) that is built to run across a wide input range. Haier, for example, lists 100 to 240 V, 50/60 Hz as the AC input for several of its current Google TVs. If your voltage stays inside your TV's rated range, the TV will run normally without a stabilizer.

Stabilizer makers argue the other side. V-Guard says an SMPS is not a stabilizer, and that long periods at very low or very high voltage push SMPS temperatures up and can shorten its life. Haier also notes that a wide voltage range is not the same as protection from sudden spikes.

**Our take:**

- **Skip it** if you live in a city flat with steady power and your TV label shows a 100 to 240 V (or wider) input range. A good surge-protected extension board is enough.
- **Buy one** if your lights dim or flicker in the evening, you see frequent power cuts with surges when power returns, you are on an inverter or generator, or your TV is older. A ₹1,500 to ₹2,200 stabilizer is cheap insurance in those homes.

Check the rating label on the back of your TV. It lists the exact input range.

## How to choose a TV stabilizer in India

### Match the TV size and load

Every TV stabilizer is sold by screen size. Add up what you will plug in: TV, set-top box, soundbar or home theatre, and console. If you are near the upper size limit, go one model up rather than running the stabilizer at its edge.

### Working voltage range

The lower number matters most in areas with evening voltage drops. All five picks start at 90 V. The Microtek EMT1390 goes up to 300 V on the high side, the widest here.

### Time delay and cut-off

Look for an initial time delay, which holds power back for a short time when supply returns after a cut, and high and low voltage cut-off. All the V-Guard and Everest models here list these.

### Warranty you can actually claim

V-Guard, Microtek and Bluechip list 3 years. Everest's ELS 100-N lists 5 years, but only 1 year of that is product warranty. Keep the invoice; most claims need it.

### Stabilizer versus surge protector

A stabilizer corrects a voltage that is too high or too low for long periods. A surge protector clamps short spikes. In a city with steady voltage, a surge-protected extension board may be all you need. Where voltage swings a lot, a stabilizer with cut-off makes more sense.
