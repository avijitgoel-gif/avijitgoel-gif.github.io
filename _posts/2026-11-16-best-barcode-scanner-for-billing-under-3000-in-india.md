## 1D laser or 2D imager: decide this first

This one choice decides what your scanner can and cannot read.

- A **1D laser scanner** sweeps a single red line across a barcode and reads the reflected light. It handles the EAN-13 and UPC codes printed on packaged goods very well, and it is the cheapest type.
- A **2D imager** is a small camera with a decoder chip. It reads the same 1D barcodes plus QR codes, Data Matrix and PDF417, in any orientation.

A laser physically cannot read a QR code, because a QR code stores data across two dimensions and a single scan line only sees one row of it. Lasers also struggle with phone and computer screens, which give off light instead of reflecting it. Coupons, loyalty cards and pickup codes now often arrive on a phone.

| What you need to scan | 1D laser | 2D imager |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Printed barcodes on packaged goods (EAN-13, UPC) | Yes | Yes |
| QR codes, Data Matrix, PDF417 | No | Yes |
| Codes shown on a phone or monitor | Poorly | Yes |
| Faded or partly damaged labels | Needs one clean line | Better, uses the whole image |
| Price under ₹3,000 | From about ₹1,200 | From about ₹1,600 |

**Our view:** if the price gap is only a few hundred rupees, buy 2D. Pick a laser only if you will scan nothing but printed product barcodes.

## Setting it up with your billing app

Nearly all USB and dongle scanners work as a "keyboard": the scanner types the barcode number into whichever field is active, then usually presses Enter. That is why they need no driver.

1. Plug in the scanner (or its wireless receiver) and open Notepad. Scan any product. If the number appears, the scanner works.
2. Open your billing software, put the cursor in the item or barcode field, and scan again.
3. If items do not add automatically, use the setup barcodes in the scanner's manual to turn on the Enter suffix.
4. For a phone or tablet, check that it supports USB OTG and use a USB OTG adapter, or buy a scanner with Bluetooth.

## Selling your own products? You need GS1 barcodes, not just a scanner

A scanner reads codes. It does not give your products official barcodes. If you make or pack products under your own brand and want to sell through retail chains or marketplaces, the 13-digit EAN barcode (GTIN) comes from GS1 India.

From 1 October 2026, GS1 India's fee for 100 barcodes for one year, for a business with turnover up to ₹5 crore, is ₹50,200. That is ₹27,000 registration, ₹13,000 annual subscription, ₹7,200 GST and a ₹3,000 refundable security deposit. GS1 India says only the brand owner, or an entity it authorises, can apply, and that the number of barcodes you choose cannot be changed later.

For loose items in your own shop, you do not need GS1 codes: most billing apps let you print internal barcode labels for stock you weigh or pack yourself.

## How to choose a barcode scanner under ₹3,000

1. **1D or 2D?** 2D if you will ever scan QR codes or phone screens. 1D only for printed product barcodes.
2. **Wired or wireless?** Wired is cheaper and never needs charging. Wireless helps with heavy items, shelves and stock counts.
3. **Bluetooth or 2.4 GHz?** A 2.4 GHz dongle needs a USB port. Bluetooth pairs directly with phones and tablets.
4. **Warranty:** ranges from 3 months to 2 years in this budget. Note who honours it and whether it is seller-specific.
5. **Rating history:** prefer hundreds of ratings, and check whether ratings are pooled across different variants.

## FAQ

### Which is the best barcode scanner for billing under Rs 3,000 in India?
The Helett HT20 at about ₹2,397 on 16 November 2026. It reads both barcodes and QR codes, works wireless or wired, has a 2,200 mAh battery and was #1 in Amazon.in's Barcode Scanners category. For a fixed counter, the wired Helett HT410 2D at about ₹1,613 is the budget pick.

### Can a laser barcode scanner read QR codes?
No. A 1D laser scanner reads along a single line, and a QR code stores data in two dimensions, so it needs a 2D imager. Laser scanners also struggle with codes shown on phone screens.

### Do barcode scanners need software or drivers?
Most USB and wireless-dongle scanners do not. They act like a keyboard and type the scanned number into the active field of your billing or inventory software.

### Can I use a barcode scanner with my phone billing app?
Yes, with either a Bluetooth scanner or a USB scanner connected through a USB OTG adapter if your phone supports OTG. The Helett HT20's wireless mode uses a 2.4 GHz USB receiver, not Bluetooth.
