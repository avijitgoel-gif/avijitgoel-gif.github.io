## What UV, MG and IR actually mean

Cheap counters advertise these letters, so it helps to know what each one does.

- **UV (ultraviolet):** the machine shines UV light on each note as it passes and flags notes that react differently from genuine currency, which catches many fakes printed on ordinary paper.
- **MG (magnetic):** the machine reads the magnetic signal from the ink and security features of each note and flags notes where it is missing or wrong.
- **IR (infrared):** the machine compares how each note looks under infrared light, a third check on top of UV and MG.

More checks is better, but no counter under ₹10,000 is foolproof. TVS says so on its own listing: the CC-232 "may not detect certain high-grade counterfeit notes (super fake notes) or taped notes" and it recommends periodic manual checks. Treat the beep as a first filter, not a final answer.

## What RBI rules mean for you if a fake note slips through

This is the money side of the decision. Under the RBI's Master Direction on Counterfeit Notes (updated 1 September 2026):

- Banks must check notes tendered over the counter using machines.
- **No credit is given** to your account for a note found to be counterfeit.
- The note is stamped "COUNTERFEIT NOTE" and impounded. It is never returned to you.
- The bank must give you an acknowledgement receipt, even if you refuse to sign it.
- Up to four fake notes in one deposit go into the bank's monthly report to the police. Five or more in a single transaction are sent to the police immediately with an FIR.

So every fake ₹500 note you accept at the counter is a straight ₹500 loss, and a bundle with five or more can bring police questions. Checking notes when you receive them, not when you deposit them, is the whole point of the machine.

## A tax rule cash businesses should know

A counting machine makes large cash receipts easy to handle, but the law caps them. From 1 April 2026, section 186 of the Income-tax Act, 2025 (which replaced section 269ST of the 1961 Act) bars receiving ₹2 lakh or more in cash from one person in a day, for one transaction, or for one event. Under section 451, the penalty can equal the full amount received. Take large payments by UPI, NEFT, RTGS or account payee cheque.

### Can a note counting machine detect all fake notes?
No. Machines under ₹10,000 catch many fakes through UV, MG and IR checks, but TVS states on its own listing that high-grade fakes and taped notes may not be detected. Check doubtful notes by hand.

### Do these machines show the total value of the cash?
No. All four machines in this guide count the number of notes, not the rupee value. Sort notes by denomination before counting, or buy a mixed value counter, which costs more than ₹10,000.

### What happens if I deposit a fake note in my bank?
Under the RBI Master Direction on Counterfeit Notes, the bank impounds the note, stamps it as counterfeit and gives no credit for it. You get an acknowledgement receipt. Five or more fake notes in one transaction are reported to the police with an FIR.

### How many notes should I put in at once?
Buyers report fewer jams with 30 to 50 notes at a time, even though the hoppers hold about 200. Flatten folded notes and remove taped or torn notes first.
