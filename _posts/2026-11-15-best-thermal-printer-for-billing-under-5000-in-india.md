## 58 mm or 80 mm: the choice that matters most

Nearly every billing printer prints at 203 DPI, which is 8 dots per millimetre. The difference is how wide the print line is:

| Paper roll | Printable width | Dots per line | Characters per line (standard font) | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 58 mm (2 inch) | 48 mm | 384 | 32 | Short bills, tea stalls, delivery, mobile billing |
| 80 mm (3 inch) | 72 mm | 576 | 48 | GST bills with many items, restaurants, supermarkets |

Thirty-two characters is enough for "item, qty, amount". It gets cramped once you add a 15-character GSTIN, HSN codes and a CGST/SGST split on every bill. If most of your bills have five or more items, or you print GST tax invoices for business customers, buy 80 mm. If you mostly print short bills from a phone, 58 mm is cheaper to buy and its rolls are cheaper too.

## What your printed GST bill must show

A printer only prints what your billing app sends it. If you are GST-registered, Rule 46 of the CGST Rules lists what a tax invoice must contain. The main items are:

- Your name, address and GSTIN
- A consecutive serial number of up to 16 characters, unique for the financial year
- Date of issue
- The buyer's name, address and GSTIN if the buyer is registered
- For an unregistered buyer, name, address and delivery address with State name and code only if the taxable value is ₹50,000 or more, or if the buyer asks for it
- HSN code, description, quantity, taxable value, tax rate and tax amount (CGST, SGST or IGST)
- Signature or digital signature of the supplier or an authorised person

This is the main reason to think about paper width. All of this fits on 58 mm, but on 80 mm it stays readable. Set up your app's invoice template once and print a test bill before your first customer.

**E-invoicing:** if your aggregate turnover crossed ₹5 crore in any year from 2017-18 onwards, B2B invoices must be reported as e-invoices from 1 August 2023 (Notification 10/2023-Central Tax), and the printed copy must carry the QR code with the Invoice Reference Number. Most small shops below that threshold do not need this.

## Thermal bills fade. Keep the digital copy

Section 36 of the CGST Act says registered businesses must keep their accounts and records for 72 months from the due date of the annual return for that year. Thermal print can fade with heat, sunlight and time, so do not rely on a paper roll copy for six years. Keep the invoice in your billing app and back it up, or export it as PDF every month. ### Should I buy a 58 mm or 80 mm thermal printer?
At the standard 203 DPI, a 58 mm printer fits about 32 characters per line and an 80 mm printer about 48. Choose 58 mm for short bills and mobile use, and 80 mm for GST invoices with many items, HSN codes and tax splits.

### Do thermal printers need ink?
No. Direct thermal printers use heat-sensitive paper, so there is no ink, toner or ribbon. The only running cost is paper rolls.

### Can I print GST bills from my phone?
Yes. A Bluetooth thermal printer can print from billing apps such as Vyapar, myBillBook and Khatabook, which the HOIN listing names as supported. The app creates the GST invoice; the printer only prints it.

### How long should I keep GST bills?
Section 36 of the CGST Act requires records to be kept for 72 months from the due date of the annual return for that year. Thermal print can fade, so keep a digital copy in your billing app or as a PDF backup.
