## The rules first: why the stamp matters more than the brand

This is the main reason to pay ₹1,300 to ₹1,900 more for a stamped scale.

### Any scale used to sell by weight must be verified and stamped

Section 24 of the Legal Metrology Act, 2009 says anyone who has a weight or measure that is used, or likely to be used, in a transaction must get it verified before putting it into use, on payment of the prescribed fee ([Legal Metrology Act, 2009, India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/4892/1/legalmetrology_act_2009.pdf)). "Transaction" includes any contract for sale or purchase, so a counter scale used to sell rice, sweets or vegetables is covered.

Section 33 sets the penalty for using an unverified weight or measure: a fine of not less than ₹2,000 that can go up to ₹10,000, and for a second or later offence, imprisonment of up to one year along with a fine. The Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act, 2026 changed many Legal Metrology penalties from 1 May 2026 so that the first offence gets an improvement notice, but Section 33 was left unchanged, so there is no warning first for an unstamped scale ([InCorpX summary of the 2026 changes](https://www.incorpx.io/blog/legal-metrology-penalties-2026-improvement-notice), [Meghalaya Legal Metrology penalty table](https://megweights.gov.in/penalties.html)).

### Electronic scales need re-verification every 12 months

Rule 27 of the Legal Metrology (General) Rules, 2011 sets the re-verification periods: 24 months for weights, capacity measures, length measures, beam scales and counter machines, 60 months for storage tanks, and 12 months for every other weight or measure ([Rule 27 text](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/19271422/)). An electronic counter scale falls in that last group.

The Legal Metrology (General) Seventh Amendment Rules, 2025, notified on 18 December 2025, rewrote the 24 month clause, but only to add petrol and diesel fuel dispensers to it ([Gazette notification G.S.R. 905(E)](https://consumeraffairs.gov.in/public/upload/files/2025.12.18%20Gen%20Rules%207th%20Amendment%202%20yr%20verification%20period_1766504014.pdf)). Electronic weighing scales were not added, so plan for a yearly visit to your local Legal Metrology office or an authorised repairer.

### The stamp is tied to the state where you use the scale

Rule 27(1) says every weight or measure used in a transaction "shall be verified and stamped by the Legal Metrology Officer in the State in which such weight or measure is put to use". All the iScale models here are packed by Baijnath Premnath in Kanpur, Uttar Pradesh. If your shop is in another state, ask your local Legal Metrology office whether the certificate in the box is enough or the scale needs fresh verification there.

Rule 27(3) and 27(4) add two more triggers: a stamped scale must be re-verified before use if it is repaired, or if it was verified in place and is then dismantled and re-installed.

### Is it legal to use an unstamped weighing scale in a shop?
No, not for selling by weight. Section 24 of the Legal Metrology Act, 2009 requires verification before a scale is used in a transaction, and Section 33 fines the use of an unverified weight or measure Rs 2,000 to Rs 10,000, with up to one year of imprisonment for a repeat offence. This section was not softened by the 2026 Jan Vishwas amendments.

### How often must an electronic shop scale be re-verified?
Every 12 months under Rule 27(2)(c) of the Legal Metrology (General) Rules, 2011. The December 2025 amendment extended the 24 month period only to weights, measures, beam scales, counter machines and fuel dispensers, not electronic scales. A repaired scale must be re-verified before use.

### Is the stamping certificate from Amazon valid in my state?
