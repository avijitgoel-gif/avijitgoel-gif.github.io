## Why the "cross-currency" fee matters in Europe

Europe is not one currency. The eurozone uses EUR, but Switzerland uses CHF, and Sweden, Denmark, Norway, Czech Republic and Poland have their own currencies. On a prepaid forex card, if you spend in a currency you did not load, the card converts from another wallet and charges a cross-currency fee:

- BookMyForex: 3.5% of the transaction value
- HDFC Multicurrency: 2%

So on a Paris, Zurich and Prague trip, a prepaid card works best if it holds EUR, CHF and CZK wallets, or if you accept the fee on the smaller amounts. HDFC's card supports SEK, DKK and NOK wallets as well, which helps on Nordic trips. Niyo has no wallets, so it simply converts INR at the Visa rate in any currency.

## Niyo Global: best for paying by card

**Best for:** travellers who pay by card for most things and visit several countries.

## FAQ

### Which forex card has zero markup for Europe?
Niyo Global charges no bank markup and uses the Visa rate. It is a debit card funded in INR, not a prepaid forex card.

### Does BookMyForex charge an issuance fee?
No. BookMyForex lists ₹0 issuance, reload and refund fees. Its cross-currency fee is 3.5%.

### What does the HDFC Multicurrency forex card cost?
Issuance is ₹500 + GST and each reload is ₹75 + GST. Cross-currency markup is 2% and ATM withdrawals cost $2 or equivalent.

### Can I use a EUR forex card in Switzerland?
You can, but you will pay a cross-currency fee (3.5% on BookMyForex, 2% on HDFC) unless you also load CHF.

### Is a forex card better than a credit card abroad?
It depends on your card's markup. A prepaid card lets you lock rates; a zero-markup card avoids markup on spends. Check your own card's fee schedule.
