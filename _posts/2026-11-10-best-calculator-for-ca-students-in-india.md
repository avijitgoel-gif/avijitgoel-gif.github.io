## What ICAI actually allows

ICAI repeats the same rule in its notes for every level. The September 2026 Foundation notes (para 14) say candidates may use a "battery operated portable calculator", which "can be of any type upto 6 functions, 12 digits and upto two memories". Anything that does not meet that spec, or has more features, "shall tantamount to use of unfair means". The notes end with one blunt line: "Scientific calculator is not allowed."

The same 6 functions, 12 digits and two memories wording appears in the guidance for CA Intermediate and CA Final. Older ICAI notes, reproduced by CharteredClub, add that printing calculators are not allowed, calculators must be noiseless and cordless, students cannot exchange calculators, and the centre superintendent can disallow any calculator that does not comply.

In practice that means:

- **12 digits is a hard ceiling.** A 14-digit model such as the Casio DJ-240D fails the rule even though it is a plain desktop calculator.
- **No scientific calculators.** The fx-82 and fx-991 series are out, however useful they were in school.
- **No phone.** ICAI says even a switched-off phone in the hall counts as unfair means.
- **ICAI names no brands.** It sets a specification and does not publish an approved model list.

### The grey area: what counts as a "function"?

ICAI does not define "function". Most students and coaching guides read it as the basic operations: add, subtract, multiply, divide, percentage and square root. Extra keys such as GT (grand total), MU (mark-up) and check and correct are common on the 12-digit models that most CA students carry, but ICAI has never listed them as allowed or banned.

**Our take:** the fewer special keys a calculator has, the less there is for an invigilator to question. A standard 12-digit model with memory, percentage and square root is the safe choice. Tax keys add more functions, so we would not make a tax-key calculator your only exam calculator. This is our reading of the rule, not ICAI's, so recheck the notes for your own attempt before exam day.

## How to choose a calculator for CA exams

### Stick to 12 digits

Count the digits on the display before you buy. Fourteen and sixteen digit models are common in the same price range and fall outside ICAI's rule.

### Look for the Indian comma marker

Casio's MJ and DJ models can show commas in the lakh and crore format. That makes ₹12,75,46,820 readable at a glance and cuts transposition errors in Accounts.

### Check and correct saves time in practice

A 120 or 150-step check lets you scroll back through a long working instead of keying it again. Whatever you choose, ICAI still expects each step of your working to be shown in the answer book.

### Practise on the calculator you will carry

Square a number with × and =, use M+ and M- for intermediate results, and clear memory before each question. Key layouts differ by brand, so do not switch models in the last month.

### Replace the battery before the exam

Solar models fall back on a small battery in dim light. Fit a fresh one a week before the exam, and check every key works.

## FAQ

### Which calculator is allowed in CA exams?
ICAI allows a battery operated portable calculator with up to 6 functions, 12 digits and up to two memories. Scientific calculators are not allowed, and a calculator with more features than this can be treated as unfair means.

### Is the Casio fx-991 allowed in CA Foundation?
No. The fx-991 is a scientific calculator, and ICAI's notes state that scientific calculators are not allowed in CA exams, including Foundation.

### Which is the best calculator for CA students in India?

### Is a check and correct calculator allowed in CA exams?
ICAI does not name models or say which keys count as functions. Twelve-digit check and correct calculators are widely carried by CA students, but the superintendent can disallow a calculator that does not comply, so a model with fewer special keys is the safest choice.

### Can I use a 14-digit calculator in the CA exam?
