### 2. The Psychology of Money by Morgan Housel: best for mindset

This is the most reviewed book in this list by a wide margin: 4.6 stars from 84,765 ratings, at ₹255 for about 252 pages ([Amazon listing](https://www.amazon.in/dp/9390166268?tag=rupeesmart01-21){:rel="sponsored nofollow noopener"}). **Watch out for:** it is not a how-to manual. It will not tell you which fund to buy or how to file tax. Pair it with an India-specific book.

**Who should buy it:** readers who want to change how they think about money before they change what they do.

## A simple reading order for beginners

1. **Let's Talk Money** for the Indian basics.
2. **The Psychology of Money** for behaviour and patience.
3. **Let's Talk Mutual Funds** before you start or review your SIPs.
4. **Coffee Can Investing** once you want to own stocks directly.
5. **The Intelligent Investor** when you are ready for a long, serious read.

If you only buy one, buy Let's Talk Money. If you buy two, add The Psychology of Money. Together they cost about ₹509 at the prices we saw.

## How to choose a personal finance book in India

### Prefer India-specific books for action

Rules on tax, insurance, provident fund and mutual funds differ by country. For anything you plan to act on, use a book written for Indian readers, then check current rules with an official source. SEBI runs a free [investor education website](https://investor.sebi.gov.in/), and the RBI publishes [financial education material](https://www.rbi.org.in/financialeducation/) for beginners.

### Use global classics for mindset

Books like The Psychology of Money and Rich Dad Poor Dad travel well because they are about behaviour, not local products.

### Check the edition and format

Amazon often shows several editions, translations and combo sets of the same title. Check the author, edition and language before you add to cart. Hindi, Marathi and Gujarati editions exist for some books in this list.

### Do not let the book replace action

The best outcome of reading is a small step this month: an emergency fund, a term insurance quote, or your first SIP.
