## What a UV lamp can and cannot check

A fake note detector in this price band is a UV tube in a small hood. It does not "decide" anything. It lights the note so you can see security features that only show under ultraviolet light. RBI lists these fluorescent features on current notes ([RBI FAQ on Indian Currency](https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/Upload/English/FAQs/PDFs/INDIANCURRENCY15042025.pdf)):

- **Number panels** are printed in fluorescent ink.
- **Dual-coloured optical fibres** are mixed into the paper.
- **The security thread** on ₹100 and higher notes fluoresces yellow on the reverse, and its text fluoresces on the front, under UV light. On ₹10, ₹20 and ₹50 notes the thread fluoresces yellow on both sides.

Several important checks need ordinary light instead, which is why a white tube is useful:

- **Watermark and electrotype watermark:** Mahatma Gandhi's portrait and the denomination numeral, seen when the note is held against light.
- **See-through register:** half the numeral is printed on each side and lines up against light.
- **Colour-shifting ink:** the numeral on ₹200 and ₹500 notes turns from green to blue when tilted.
- **Micro lettering:** "भारत" and "India", best seen with a magnifying glass, which is why the KUSAM-MECO's built-in lens is handy.

RBI's Paisa Bolta Hai site lists 17 front and back features for the current ₹500 note, including the green-to-blue windowed thread and the Red Fort on the reverse ([RBI Paisa Bolta Hai](https://paisaboltahai.rbi.org.in/rupees-five-hundred.aspx)). The habit that works: check every ₹500 and ₹200 under UV, then hold doubtful ones against the white light for the watermark and tilt for the colour shift.

**A note on "magnetic" claims.** Some listings in this band, including the swaggers Compact and swaggers Mini UV, name "Magnetic" under detection technology while their specification lists only a UV lamp and no sensor or indicator. Treat these as UV lamps. If you want machine checks of magnetic and infrared features, you need a note counting machine with MG and IR sensors, which costs more (see our [note counting machine guide](/best-note-counting-machine-under-10000-in-india/)).

## What happens if a fake note gets past you

Under the RBI Master Direction on Counterfeit Notes, banks must examine notes through machines, give no credit for any counterfeit found, stamp it "COUNTERFEIT NOTE" and impound it, and issue you an acknowledgement receipt even if you refuse to sign. If five or more fake notes turn up in a single transaction, the bank's nodal officer must forward them to the police for an FIR ([RBI](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=13361&Mode=0)).

So the person who loses the money is whoever accepts the fake note last. Checking at the counter is the only point where you can still refuse it.

### Can a UV fake note detector catch every fake note?
No. A UV lamp only shows fluorescent features such as the number panels, optical fibres and security thread. Also check the watermark and see-through register against light, and tilt Rs 200 and Rs 500 notes to see the numeral change from green to blue.

### What glows on a genuine Rs 500 note under UV light?
According to RBI, the number panels are printed in fluorescent ink, the paper has dual-coloured optical fibres, and the security thread fluoresces yellow on the reverse with its text fluorescing on the front.

### What happens if I deposit a fake note in my bank?
The bank gives no credit for it, stamps it as counterfeit, impounds it and gives you a receipt. If five or more fake notes are found in one transaction, the bank sends them to the police for an FIR.

### Is a fake note detector better than a note counting machine?
They do different jobs. A Rs 1,000 UV detector helps you check single notes by eye. A note counting machine with UV, MG and IR sensors checks every note automatically while counting, but costs Rs 5,000 or more.
